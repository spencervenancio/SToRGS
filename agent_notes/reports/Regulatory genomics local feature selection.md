# Give each sequence its own error rate

Tonight's next step is a pre-registered calibration audit on the DFIM planted-motif benchmark, which holds 60,000 synthetic 200-bp sequences with recorded positions of cooperating ELF1 and SIX5 motifs and of label-independent AP1/TAL1 decoys. Train a small CNN, cut each held-out sequence into ten 20-bp tiles, and measure, sequence by sequence and separately for each kind of irrelevant tile, whether three kinds of rule actually control false selections: the field's pooled shuffle-null "FDR" threshold, the thesis's fixed relative cutoff, and a per-sequence conditional randomization test (CRT) that redraws each tile from the exact background and applies Benjamini–Hochberg or Benjamini–Yekutieli within that one sequence. The student's first concern is right but aims at the wrong gap: attribution maps, seqlets and motif hits are already per-sequence, and what no surveyed tool provides is an error statement for the set selected in one sequence against a stated conditional null — tangermeme's own documentation says its seqlet p-values "are not error rates." The second concern dissolves once ground truth is indexed by estimand: planted motifs are exact truth about the generating function and back calibration claims, exhaustive ablation of the trained model is exact truth about the model and backs faithfulness claims, and mutagenesis assays are noisy truth about biology, for later. The design transplants the thesis's best-confirmed lesson — the two-stage decoy rule passed all five registered claims yet controls only never-used features — by mapping never-used features to background tiles and inactive branches to lone or redundant motif copies, so every outcome triggers a decision written before the run. Compute is roughly 30–60 minutes on the M3 (extrapolated from exploratory probes, not measured results), the DFIM route needs no new packages in the repo's virtualenv, and Slurm can wait for ChromBPNet-scale models. Two cautions travel into the meeting: novelty is unverified, because neither literature search was exhaustive for 2025–2026 preprints and the general-ML version of this test dates to 2019; and three repo defects — missing `lfs/data` modules, a `data/` ignore rule that silently swallows new loaders, and an environment mismatch with the probes — must be handled before coding starts.

## Every pipeline already selects per sequence; none bounds the errors

Step 1 of the 10-12 plan is met without argument: attribution maps are regulatory genomics' central interpretability object, and each is already a per-sequence, per-output score. Models read DNA one-hot encoded, as a 4 × L binary matrix with a single 1 per position, and an attribution assigns each base a share of one prediction. BPNet explains its profile head with DeepLIFT against an all-zeros reference, chosen because it "showed the highest correlation with in-silico mutagenesis contribution scores" ([Avsec et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8812996/)); ChromBPNet averages DeepLIFT/SHAP over **20 dinucleotide-shuffled references** per input, shuffles that reorder a sequence while preserving its counts of adjacent base pairs ([Pampari et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11741299/)); DeepSTARR uses 100 ([code](https://github.com/bernardo-de-almeida/DeepSTARR/blob/main/DeepSTARR/DeepSTARR_nucl_contr_scores.py)); Enformer uses gradient×input and in-silico mutagenesis (ISM), which mutates every base and re-predicts ([Avsec et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8490152/)). The binary step is per-sequence as well. Transcription factors (TFs) bind short DNA words called motifs, usually summarized as a position weight matrix (PWM) of per-position base probabilities; TF-MoDISco cuts high-scoring stretches of attribution, called seqlets, out of each sequence in turn ([TF-MoDISco note](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00416)), and Fi-NeMo calls individual motif instances, or hits, region by region ([Fi-NeMo](https://github.com/kundajelab/Fi-NeMo)). The student is right that adapting a global method to be local is not a contribution, and for a stronger reason than locality: producing a per-sequence set is not new either.

What is missing is any error statement about those sets. tfmodisco-lite concatenates window scores from **all** sequences before choosing one threshold, estimates its "FDR" by isotonic regression against a Laplace curve fitted to the same observed scores, targets 0.2 by default, and lets caps on the passing fraction (3%–20%) override that target ([extract_seqlets.py](https://github.com/jmschrei/tfmodisco-lite/blob/main/modiscolite/extract_seqlets.py); [tfmodisco.py](https://github.com/jmschrei/tfmodisco-lite/blob/main/modiscolite/tfmodisco.py)). tangermeme is blunter than any critic: its seqlet p-values "rank spans; they are not error rates," and "a threshold of 0.01 does not mean that 1% of the calls are false" ([seqlet.py](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/blob/main/tangermeme/seqlet.py)). Fi-NeMo's hits are the support of a non-negative lasso with a default penalty of 0.7, are explicitly not nested across penalty values ("Parameters should be selected holistically"), and carry no significance statement at all ([README](https://github.com/kundajelab/Fi-NeMo)). The stage map condenses the landscape survey: every error-like number is a dataset average or a heuristic, and every null concerns the fitted model's behavior on shuffled or random DNA, never the rest of the sequence being explained.

Step 2, whether the 0/1 question is useful there, resolves just as clearly, because the field's headline biology consumes discrete calls. BPNet's ~10.5-bp helical spacing preference and its motif co-occurrence tests are computed over mapped instances ([Avsec et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8812996/)); Fi-NeMo writes a table of "the number of hits of each motif in each input region" ([README](https://github.com/kundajelab/Fi-NeMo)); tangermeme ships pairwise spacing statistics over annotated seqlets ([annotate.py](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/blob/main/tangermeme/annotate.py)). A false instance leaks straight into spacing histograms, motif counts and the choice of which site to mutate at the bench, and neither the landscape nor the ground-truth researcher found any source that measures the false-discovery proportion of a hit caller. The meeting notes' own motivation, that practitioners "make their own thresholding decisions based on heuristics" (meeting notes, 10-12 (senior-thesis: `notes/meeting_notes.tex`)), is documented by the tools' authors themselves.

The precise gap is a **calibration layer between attribution and hit calling**. For one sequence x and a fixed trained model f̂, return a set of motif-sized windows with a per-sequence guarantee E[V(x)/max(R(x), 1)] ≤ q — V(x) false windows among R(x) selected — against an explicit local null: redrawing window W from a stated background, given its flanks x₋W, leaves the model's output unchanged in distribution. Four features make this more than a port. The unit must be a group, because positions inside a motif are jointly necessary and attributions are "correlated along the sequence" ([tangermeme](https://github.com/jmschrei/tangermeme/blob/main/tangermeme/seqlet.py)). Redundant copies defeat one-at-a-time tests: ISM "fails to highlight important features masked by saturation… multiple motif instances in a sequence that buffer each other" ([Greenside et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6129272/)). The local value function is not monotone; in CREME's Enformer experiments "necessity does not imply sufficiency," and regulatory elements compete ([Toneyan & Koo preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.07.03.547592)). And every existing guarantee-like number concerns f̂, so the claim must say whether it is about the model or the biology.

The statistics themselves are not new, and the thesis should not claim they are. Burns, Thomason and Tansey already "reframe black box model interpretability as a multiple hypothesis testing problem," with randomization p-values for feature subsets of one input and Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) across them ([arXiv:1904.00045](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00045)); SHAP-XRT and testing by betting give local conditional tests for fixed models ([Teneggi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07038); [Teneggi & Sulam 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19146)). What the notes found open is the genomic instantiation — exact DNA samplers, window groups, redundancy-aware estimands and validation by null type against planted truth — and even that is provisional. The landscape researcher states that the search for 2025–2026 preprints proposing per-sequence FDR on genomic attributions was not exhaustive, and the statistics researcher defines "apparently open" as "not found." The notes also disagree on the nearest precedent: the landscape note names ChromBPNet's variant p-values and misses the AlphaGenome sham-deletion study that the statistics note found, which tests each motif in its own context but applies no multiplicity correction, no planted benchmark and only qualitative discussion of redundancy ([Liang et al. 2026](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/27/4/bbag422/8753614)); DFIM, meanwhile, applies BH to shuffle-null interaction scores over a family it never specifies ([Greenside et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6129272/)). The defensible meeting claim is therefore the narrow one — validated per-sequence error control for motif-instance selection, broken down by null type — preceded by a 30-minute targeted bioRxiv and arXiv search. Framed that way it meets the advisor's bar from 09-28, that a method "has any bones" if "it beats the state of the art" or there is "some nice theory" (meeting notes (senior-thesis: `notes/meeting_notes.tex`)): the CRT brings a finite-sample guarantee, and tonight tests whether it beats the field's procedure where it matters, per sequence.

## Three kinds of ground truth back three different claims

Usable ground truth exists, but the phrase names three different estimands, and conflating them is the real danger. **Planted motifs** give the exact support of the generating function in each sequence, S\*_gen(x), for simplified grammars only; simulation keeps one "in control of the exact ground truth," while on real data "the ground truth is not definitively known" ([Prakash et al. 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v165/prakash22a/prakash22a.pdf)). **Exact model ablation** — deleting or mutating parts of x and re-running f̂ — is noise-free and local but silent about biology, and accuracy does not buy faithful explanations: "CNNs with better test performance do not necessarily imply more interpretable representations" ([Koo & Ploenzke 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8315445)). **Assays** measure the effect of specific edits in a specific cell type. A massively parallel reporter assay (MPRA) counts barcoded RNA against DNA for thousands of constructs; Kircher et al.'s saturation-mutagenesis MPRA, which tests single-base changes across whole elements, covers 21 regulatory elements and **31,243 variant effects**, its best model correlated with the measurements at only 0.22 Spearman ([Kircher et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6687891/)), and per-element replicate correlations run from 0.55 for one MYC enhancer to 0.99 for LDLR ([CAGI5](https://genomeinterpretation.ccs.neu.edu/cagi5-regulation-saturation.html)). DeepSTARR's oligo libraries add what single edits cannot see — each motif mutated one instance at a time and all instances at once, the only redundancy-aware experimental truth the notes found — and there model-predicted and measured importance of 1,013 GATA instances correlate at 0.53 ([de Almeida et al. bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.10.05.463203v1.full)).

| Claim the thesis wants to make | Truth that can support it | Data | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| The rule controls per-sequence FDR at q | planted background windows, under a sampler that matches how the background was generated | DFIM tonight; the thesis's own generator next | tonight |
| The rule recovers the generating support | S\*_gen(x) with the estimand declared: necessary instances, or the union of all copies | DFIM; own generator | tonight, then week 1 |
| The selection is faithful to the model | exhaustive ablation of f̂: ISM, or every subset of planted instances | any trained model | tonight on planted instances; later at scale |
| The selected instances matter biologically | single- vs all-instance knockouts, saturation mutagenesis, CRISPR edits | DeepSTARR libraries; Kircher portal | weeks out, and never for calibration |

Three traps sit between these truths and a usable estimand. Benchmark masks are not local supports: the Koo-lab and Prakash evaluations mark every planted position of a label-associated motif and score positive sequences only — the Koo-lab scripts take the first 500 positive test sequences ([helper.py](https://github.com/p-koo/GradientCorrection/blob/main/gradient_correction/helper.py)) — which rewards a union-of-copies notion and penalizes a necessary-instance notion, and the two diverge exactly under redundancy, where "perturbing either… will not produce a change in the output" ([DeepLIFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02685)). The most-used dataset is internally inconsistent: Majdandzic et al. describe Koo Task 3 positives as carrying 3–5 core motifs and negatives as drawn from 100 background motifs ([Genome Biology 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10169356/)), but the generating notebook, read and not re-run, allows 3–7 motifs and places the 10 core PWMs inside the negatives' pool ([task3 notebook](https://github.com/p-koo/exponential_activations/blob/master/code/generate_data/task3_generate_synthetic_code_dataset.ipynb)), so a planted motif in a negative is not a safe null. And the advisor's framework needs translating. In the repo's reading of the Null Importance paper ([Raskutti, Sankaran & Ye, arXiv:2609.19511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19511)), saliency targets the pointwise null ∂f/∂x_j = 0, but one-hot DNA lives on the vertices of a simplex, so derivatives are not identified by data and only finite changes between sequences are. The genomic estimand must be a finite-change "replace this window" null with three dials declared: the replacement distribution, the output scale (logit, probability or loss), and necessity versus union. The repo identifies that paper as the one the advisor sent only by inference, and no note read its Sections 5.2–5.3, so whether it already proposes a pointwise test is unverified.

The DFIM simulation is tonight's best fit because one download holds every structure the thesis cares about. It contains 60,000 sequences of 200 bp on a random background with 0.46 GC content: class 1 carries 1–2 ELF1 instances, class 2 carries 1–2 SIX5, class 3 carries both, AP1 and TAL1 are embedded 0 or 1 times independently of class, and task 3 is the ELF1-AND-SIX5 gate ([Greenside et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6129272/)). The ground-truth researcher verified exact start positions and planted strings per sequence and decoys balanced across classes — in class 1, 5,048 sequences with no decoy, 5,043 with AP1, 4,971 with both and 4,938 with TAL1 ([kundajelab/dfim](https://github.com/kundajelab/dfim)) — and a HEAD request made while writing this report found the three files live on the repository's `master` branch at 3.6, 0.15 and 4.2 MB. An AND gate whose support switches between sequences, redundant copies, lone partners and true decoys all sit on an i.i.d. background, which makes an exact conditional sampler trivial. The repository lists no license, so the files stay in the gitignored `data/` and are never redistributed; its shipped Keras model is not needed. The Koo `synthetic_code_dataset.h5` remains the fallback: it downloads in seconds and a CNN reached test AUROC 0.980 on it in 28.6 s in an exploratory probe ([train_probe.py](<../research_notes/Regulatory genomics local feature selection/tooling_probes/train_probe.py>)), but its truth supports only the background stratum.

## Tonight: ten tiles per sequence, four rules, one registered decision

### DFIM carries the thesis's null taxonomy into DNA

Train one multi-task CNN in the Majdandzic "CNN-deep" shape — convolutions of 24×19, 32×7, 48×7 and 64×3 with pooling and batch norm, a 96-unit dense layer, three sigmoid heads — with Adam at 10⁻³, batch 100, up to 30 epochs with early stopping, on a fixed 70/10/20 split, for three seeds ([Majdandzic et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10169356/)). Study two heads as a two-member function family in the sense of the repo's 24-function, 8-family benchmark. The presence head (task 1, "any ELF1") is the redundancy case: its support is the sole ELF1 copy, and no single instance is necessary when two copies buffer each other. The AND head (task 3) is the conditional case, with a point-dependent support exactly like `conditional_interaction`'s `local_support(x)`: both partners in class-3 sequences, and **the empty set in the two-thirds of sequences that carry only one partner**. Cut each held-out sequence into ten non-overlapping 20-bp tiles (TF-MoDISco's default core window is 21 bp) and give each (sequence, head, tile) a truth code computed exactly from the metadata, by deleting the instances the tile overlaps and re-evaluating the head's label rule. The codes translate `lfs/metrics/local_nulls.py` into DNA; the second column is what the thesis already measured for the same null type (null-reference REPORT (senior-thesis: `results/saliency_threshold_null_reference/REPORT.md`)).

| Thesis null type | `decoy@0.1` selection rate, continuous benchmark | DFIM counterpart | Covered by the CRT guarantee? |
|---|---|---|---|
| never used, independent inputs | 0.098 (nominal 0.1) | background tile: no planted instance, an i.i.d. draw | yes, exactly |
| never used, correlated or structured | 0.135 | decoy tile: AP1 or TAL1 for both heads; SIX5 for the presence head | no; held only if f̂ ignores it |
| inactive branch | 0.484 | lone ELF1 or SIX5 under the AND head; one of two copies of a used motif (coded "redundant") | no |
| gate | 0.408 | none in DFIM; under a finite-change estimand a regime switch changes the output when removed, so it becomes a recall target | not applicable |

The table states the thesis's prediction before the run. On the continuous benchmark a measured null "delivers its guarantee exactly, on the nulls it models" and nothing more (null-reference LAB_NOTE (senior-thesis: `experiment_design/saliency_threshold_null_reference/LAB_NOTE.md`)); the two-stage decoy rule (global screen at α 0.05, local selection at α 0.2, 19 decoys) then passed all five registered claims on fresh seeds, with F1 **0.814** against 0.761 for the fixed threshold and a selection rate of **0.018** on never-used features, yet on local-support functions it fell to F1 0.577 with FDR 0.482 while the fixed threshold scored best at 0.644 (confirmation REPORT (senior-thesis: `results/saliency_threshold_two_stage_confirmation/REPORT.md`); registered claims (senior-thesis: `results/saliency_two_stage_confirmation_claims/REPORT.md`)). DNA breaks that rule's mechanics, not its logic. Appended N(0,1) decoy columns are not valid one-hot input, and a CNN's shared filters violate the column-symmetric training that the decoy exchangeability argument assumes (null_reference.py (senior-thesis: `lfs/selection/null_reference.py`)). The per-tile CRT keeps the formula of `decoy_pvalues`, p = (1 + #{redraws ≥ observed})/(K + 1), but draws its decoys inside the sequence from the background distribution, so validity needs only a held-out test sequence and a correct sampler. The gate row changes meaning too: deleting a regime-switching motif changes the output, which puts it in the thesis's structural `local_support` rather than its `local_effect_support` — the target-definition choice that moved `conditional_interaction` F1 from 0.716 to 0.536 on the continuous benchmark (local-effect LAB_NOTE (senior-thesis: `results/saliency_threshold_all_designs_local_effect/LAB_NOTE.md`)).

### Four selection rules, two guaranteed on background tiles

| Arm | Statistic per tile | Cutoff | Error claim | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fixed_rel@t*` | absolute tile sum of off-simplex-corrected gradient×input, divided by the sequence maximum | u ≥ t\*, with t\* tuned for F1 on validation | none | the thesis's incumbent heuristic (`select_threshold` family) |
| `pooled_shuffle@q` | the same tile sums | one cutoff over all test tiles, where control tiles from 5 dinucleotide shuffles per sequence give estimated FDR ≤ q | dataset-average estimate | the field's FDR mode, reimplemented |
| `crt_logit@α`, `crt_logit_bh@q`, `crt_logit_by@q` | two-sided distance of the head's logit from the mean over the observed tile and K = 999 redraws | per tile at α, the `decoy@α` analogue; BH within the sequence; Benjamini–Yekutieli (BY) within the sequence | level α per tile; FDR ≤ q on background tiles under positive dependence (BH) or any dependence (BY) | the candidate method |
| `crt_saliency_bh@q` | the tile's saliency sum, recomputed on each redraw (K = 199; seed 0, AND head) | BH within the sequence | as BH | the thesis's own statistic, calibrated per sequence |

All CRT arms share one engine. For each test sequence and tile, redraw the tile's 20 bases i.i.d. from the background composition — A and T at 0.27, C and G at 0.23 if the stated 0.46 GC is strand-symmetric, which a preflight on unembedded training positions must confirm, together with the absence of dinucleotide dependence, before the test split is opened — then run the model and turn the K + 1 statistics into a p-value. Levels are q ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2} with 0.1 primary, the same levels as the two-stage line. K follows from resolution, not taste: a K-draw p-value cannot fall below 1/(K + 1), BH over ten tiles needs p ≤ q/10, and BY at q = 0.05 needs p ≤ 0.0017, so K = 999. Each (sequence, tile) gets a child stream from `lfs.seed.spawn`, the run stays single-process, and the CRT, like `loco`, takes a fitted model — a deliberate exception to the unfitted-estimator convention that its docstring should state. The field arm uses tangermeme's `dinucleotide_shuffle` only if a dry-run install into the repo venv leaves torch and numpy untouched; otherwise it uses a short numpy port of the doublet-preserving Euler shuffle ([uShuffle](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2375906)), and never simdna's, which the tooling probe found broken under Python 3 ([bench.py](<../research_notes/Regulatory genomics local feature selection/tooling_probes/bench.py>)). DeepLIFT/SHAP with per-sequence shuffled references, the ChromBPNet standard, is a follow-up arm.

### Every registered outcome already has a decision attached

Score every arm per sequence, never only pooled: the per-sequence false-discovery proportion counting background tiles as false, averaged over sequences (FDR_bg, the quantity the CRT guarantees, and an accounting generous to the field because decoy and inactive selections are not charged); the selection rate of each null type (the `pfer_<type>` quantity of `local_nulls.py`); power on necessary tiles and on the field's union mask; and per-sequence FWER. Stratify by class and by tertile of each sequence's maximum tile attribution, with sequence-bootstrap intervals pooled hierarchically over seeds as the existing runners do, Bonferroni-adjusted across the strata each rule names. Register the split seed and a class-balanced test subsample of 1,500 sequences (500 per class) before anything runs, and reserve a disjoint subsample for confirmation, which is DFIM's version of the repo's fresh-seed rule.

| G0: test AUROC ≥ 0.95 for both heads on every seed | interpret that head | report the head as inadequate and draw no claims from it, as the corrected-classifier Stage A did |
| G1: `crt_logit@0.1` rejects ≤ 0.11 of background tiles, and `crt_logit_by@0.1` keeps FDR_bg's upper bound ≤ 0.1 in every class | the engine is trusted | a bug in the sampler, the split or the p-values; fix it and rerun on the reserved subsample |
| C1: `pooled_shuffle@0.1` has FDR_bg with lower bound > 0.1 among AND-head negatives or in the lowest signal tertile | the per-sequence gap is real in the field's own procedure; the thesis contribution is the calibrated selection layer | the pooled rule already holds per sequence here; shift the thesis's weight to the local-null and estimand question (C2) |
| C2: `crt_logit_bh@0.1` selects inactive or redundant tiles at a rate with lower bound > 0.1 | local nulls stay uncontrolled in DNA, as on the continuous benchmark; the next method targets them with region-level and two-stage tests | f̂ learned sharp logic on this benchmark; stress it with leakier models and harder grammars before believing control transfers |

The predicted pattern is G1, C1 and C2 holding, which would let the meeting open on one sentence: on the field's own benchmark, the pooled seqlet-style "FDR" does not hold sequence by sequence, a per-sequence CRT does on the nulls it models, and lone or redundant motif copies — the genomic face of the thesis's inactive-branch problem — stay uncontrolled by both. That is a prediction, anchored in the boundary analysis's finding that "a feature the model uses somewhere keeps gradient that is small relative to the actives but far above a never-used feature's noise," so that "'more than noise' and 'a material share of the local effect' are different questions; a local claim needs both" (boundary LAB_NOTE (senior-thesis: `experiment_design/saliency_threshold_two_stage_boundary/LAB_NOTE.md`)). The CRT answers the first question; C2 measures how far it is from the second. A skeptical labmate will raise three objections, and the design answers each. The background guarantee is nearly a theorem in a simulation with a known background, so G1 is an implementation check and the findings are C1 and C2. The field uses DeepLIFT and variable-length seqlets rather than corrected gradient×input on fixed tiles, but what is being tested is the pooled control-FDR estimator, and tangermeme's own `recursive_seqlets` is the first follow-up arm. A max-normalized cutoff cannot return the empty set, so it loses on negatives by construction — which is the point, because that rule is the thesis's own `select_threshold`, and in a domain where nothing is the right answer for two-thirds of AND-head sequences, only a calibrated test can say so.

### Both open problems become measured strata

Non-monotonicity appears in DFIM without any planted repressor, as soon as the thesis's convention that importance is an increase in loss is applied at a single sequence. For a true negative of the AND head, a lone ELF1 that nudges the predicted probability upward makes the per-sample loss worse, so redrawing that tile lowers the loss and its importance is negative. That is CLAUDE.md's open problem 1 in biological form — half of a cooperative pair worsening the prediction at x — and the run keeps it visible twice. The CRT recomputed on the per-sample loss (passed as `loss=` from `lfs.metrics.pointwise`, cross-entropy on probabilities) is two-sided and reports signed effects, counting how many inactive tiles carry significantly negative importance, with nothing clamped. An ablation lattice enumerates all 2^k subsets of each sequence's planted instances (k ≤ 6, 32 background redraws per subset) and records each instance's exact minimum marginal contribution over subsets, the quantity MinShap estimates from K random orderings. The repo predicts what it will show: in the gate controls MinShap selected the pure-synergy pair in 0 of 5 runs while the mean aggregator caught it in 5 of 5 (gate controls (senior-thesis: `results/gate_controls_analysis/REPORT.md`)), so the minimum should sit at or below zero for AND partners and redundant copies alike, with genuine negatives reported raw. DFIM has no repressor motif, so the week-1 generator adds one as a designed stratum.

Threshold calibration gets a precise but scoped answer. The CRT turns the statistic the thesis has been thresholding heuristically — saliency — into a per-sequence threshold with a finite-sample guarantee relative to the background sampler, while `select_threshold` stays in the comparison as the admitted heuristic rather than being patched. The guarantee is about f̂: if this tile had been drawn from the background, a change in the prediction this large would be rare. It transfers to the generating function only on background tiles, and only because in simulation those tiles really are draws from the sampler, which is why calibration claims must rest on synthetic data. The meeting notes define a level-α threshold as a probability statement that the statistics researcher reads as being over training randomness (meeting notes, 09-21 (senior-thesis: `notes/meeting_notes.tex`)); three seeds tonight measure how selections move across retraining, reported as tile-set Jaccard, and do not control it — the notes' own warning that "measuring it isn't the same as controlling it."

The Markov step carries an unverified ingredient. Under a k-th-order Markov background a window depends on the rest of the sequence only through its k flanking bases on each side, so forward–backward bridge sampling is exact; the statistics researcher's further claim that a uniform doublet-preserving shuffle of the window with fixed endpoints is also an exact conditional sampler is that researcher's own derivation ("I found no paper that states it"), so it must be proven or checked by simulation before it carries a guarantee. Robustness bounds exist for the mismatched case — conditional-permutation type I error is bounded in terms of the error in approximating X | Z ([Berrett et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.05405)), and knockoff FDR inflation is proportional to the error in estimating each feature's conditional distribution ([Barber et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03896)) — and genomic language models are no better as samplers, since their frozen embeddings showed no clear advantage over one-hot models on regulatory tasks ([Tang et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12261763/)). The hierarchical step borrows KnockoffZoom's rule that a contiguous group is null only if all its subgroups are ([Sesia et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7046731/)) and TreeBH's dependence-robust variant ([Bogomolov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07529)); the honest answer for two buffering copies is "this region, not this copy." The real-data step has known friction. DeepSTARR's model is a legacy TensorFlow 1.14/Keras 2.2.4 file with no official PyTorch port and its README links two GEO accessions inconsistently ([DeepSTARR repo](https://github.com/bernardo-de-almeida/DeepSTARR)), while the bioRxiv text gives enhancer counts of 804/872 in one place and 855/905 in its Methods ([de Almeida et al. bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.10.05.463203v1.full)). Kircher's data are shared under University of Washington terms and the Fort Lauderdale principle, which should be read before use ([satMutMPRA portal](https://kircherlab.bihealth.org/satMutMPRA/)). ChromBPNet models for 1,512 ENCODE datasets load into PyTorch through bpnet-lite installed with `--no-deps`, because its macs3 dependency has no Mac wheel; DeepLIFT/SHAP took 2.5 s per sequence on MPS in an exploratory probe, but ISM is estimated, not measured, at 11–30 hours per 1,000 sequences on the laptop, which is the job for Slurm, as is Fi-NeMo, whose pyBigWig dependency also lacks a PyPI Mac wheel.

## Conclusion

The pivot relocates the thesis rather than restarting it. Its hardest-won result — a measured null controls exactly the nulls it models and nothing else — becomes, in DNA, the dividing line between what a per-sequence test can promise (background) and what remains research (lone and redundant copies of motifs that matter elsewhere), and genomics supplies two things the continuous benchmark never had: biological names for the uncontrolled nulls, cooperativity and redundancy, and an experimental adjudicator for them in single- versus all-instance knockouts. Two things genuinely change. The empty set becomes a common correct answer, for two-thirds of AND-head sequences, and a max-normalized threshold cannot express it, so per-sequence calibration stops being a refinement and becomes the price of any per-sequence claim. And the estimand acquires three explicit dials — replacement distribution, output scale, necessity versus union — whose settings turn open problem 1 from a nuisance into a measurable stratum, since under the thesis's own loss convention half of a cooperative pair is predicted to make a true negative's prediction worse.

Two consequences are easy to miss. The field's tool authors already concede the gap in writing — "not error rates," "parameters should be selected holistically" — so a calibrated per-sequence layer arrives as something practitioners lack rather than a critique they must absorb, and it slots between tools they already run. And because every outcome of tonight's run is bound to a decision before the test subsample opens, the meeting can be about what to do next whichever way the numbers fall; if they fall as predicted, the uncontrolled remainder is the thesis's own open local problem with biological names attached.
